\section{What a fixed-reasoning gradient can and cannot see}
\label{sec:theory}

An edit $P\to P'$ changes accuracy $J$ through the reasoning the model writes and through how an
answer is read off a given reasoning. With the likelihood ratio
$w(r)=\pi_{P'}(r\mid x)/\pi_P(r\mid x)$ and $\mathbb{E}_{\pi_P}[w\,g]=\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{P'}}[g]$,
exactly (for $\tau>0$, where every reasoning has positive probability under both prompts),
\begin{equation}
J(P')-J(P)=\underbrace{\mathbb{E}_{x}\mathbb{E}_{r\sim\pi_P}\big[(w(r)-1)\,c_{P'}(x,r)\big]}_{D\text{: distribution term}}
+\underbrace{\mathbb{E}_{x}\mathbb{E}_{r\sim\pi_P}\big[c_{P'}(x,r)-c_P(x,r)\big]}_{A\text{: read-off term}} .
\label{eq:decomp}
\end{equation}
The fixed-reasoning objective (Eq.~\ref{eq:greater}) is a soft version of $A$ on a single
reasoning, the incumbent's greedy one, and does not measure $D$. To first order,
$D\approx\operatorname{Cov}_{\pi_P}(\log w,\,c)$: an edit helps if it makes the model's own
\emph{correct} reasoning more likely relative to its incorrect reasoning---the score-function term
that a gradient over a fixed reasoning drops. If reading is faithful (answers read under $P'$ and
$P$ differ only on reasonings of $\pi_P$-probability at most $\varepsilon$), then
$J(P')-J(P)=D\pm\varepsilon$; on a question whose reasoning supports a wrong answer, every decrease
of the objective raises the probability of reading an answer the reasoning does not support
(Proposition~\ref{prop:faithful}, Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}).

\paragraph{The read-off term is small; the distribution moves far.} On the incumbents' own samples,
the hard read of a fixed reasoning changes under a candidate in only $0.0$--$3.2\%$ of (candidate,
question, sample) triples across the eleven pools. The absolute read-off term averages $0.0$--$1.2$
accuracy points per edit against mean absolute held-out effects of $2.1$--$10.9$, at most about a
third of the effect in any pool, and correlates with the held-out change at $-0.31$ to $+0.51$ across
pools (pooled $+0.018$; Tables~\ref{tab:readoff} and~\ref{tab:hdist}). The distribution, by contrast,
moves far: $\mathrm{KL}(\pi_P\,\|\,\pi_{P'})=-\mathbb{E}_{\pi_P}[\log w]$ has a median of $1.1$--$14.6$ nats per reasoning across pools (Table~\ref{tab:readoff}): the
reasoning a fixed-reasoning gradient is taken over is rarely one the edited prompt would write.

\paragraph{The natural repair: reweight the model's own samples.} With \greater{}'s white-box
access, $D$ can be estimated without new generation: draw $S$ samples
$r_s\sim\pi_P^\tau(\cdot\mid x)$ once per training question, read them under the incumbent, and
compute the exact log-ratios $\log w_s$ of the \emph{same} samples under every candidate. The
tempered self-normalized importance sampling (SNIS) estimate
\begin{equation}
\widehat{J}_\beta(P'\mid x)=\frac{\sum_s w_s^{\beta}\,c_P(x,r_s)}{\sum_s w_s^{\beta}},\qquad
\widehat{\Delta}_\beta(P')=\mathbb{E}_x\big[\widehat{J}_\beta(P'\mid x)-\tfrac1S\textstyle\sum_s c_P(x,r_s)\big]
\label{eq:snis}
\end{equation}
estimates $D$ (with the two reads exchanged), and the full change with candidate reads $c_{P'}$ in
the numerator. At $\beta=0$ it is the read-off term $A$ on the incumbent's samples (candidate reads)
or a sampled analogue of the fixed-reasoning objective (soft reads); $\beta=1$ is consistent as
$S\to\infty$; for small $\beta$, $\widehat\Delta_\beta\approx\beta\,\widehat{\operatorname{Cov}}(\log
w,c)$. Because weights
over hundreds of tokens are heavy-tailed, $\beta$ is chosen \emph{without labels} as the largest
value in $\{1,\tfrac12,\tfrac14\}$ whose median effective sample size
$(\sum_s w_s^\beta)^2/\sum_s w_s^{2\beta}$ is at least $S/4$. No reasoning is generated under any
candidate.

\paragraph{Coverage predicts that reweighting fails.} Importance sampling needs about
$\exp(\mathrm{KL}(\pi_{P'}\|\pi_P))$ samples \citep{chatterjee2018sample}; incumbent samples give
only the reverse divergence, so its comparison with $\log16\approx2.8$ nats (exceeded by the median
in $10$ of $11$ pools) is indicative, and the effective sample size at $\beta=1$, $1.4$--$5.1$ of
$16$, shows the shortfall directly. Tempering trades coverage for bias toward the fixed-reasoning
family, and self-normalization hides the failure (Appendix~\ref{app:dist}). The prediction is
that no $\beta$ yields a rank signal. It was written after five of the eleven pools had been observed
(Appendix~\ref{app:prereg}); the registered H-dist test, fixed before any of its data, asks whether
this principled gradient over the distribution of reasoning beats a random shortlist
(\S\ref{sec:exp-validity}).
