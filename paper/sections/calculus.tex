\section{Exact structural edits in one sequence}
\label{sec:calculus}

\paragraph{Typed prompts and edits.} A prompt is a sequence of typed blocks $P=(b_1,\dots,b_m)$
(task, strategy, procedure, output, input, example); editable blocks may be empty insertion
slots. The edit space of slot $j$ is a candidate set $C_j$ (rewrites, or new blocks for an empty
slot) plus deletion. Candidates are proposed by the task model itself from unlabeled training
questions with type-conditioned operators (Appendix~\ref{app:details}).

\paragraph{Superposed sequence.} For one question we build a single token sequence holding the
incumbent prompt $P(x)$, the tokens of every candidate of every slot, and a scored tail (the
fixed reasoning and answer of Eq.~\ref{eq:greater}, or a sampled reasoning, \S\ref{sec:method}).
Candidate tokens of slot $j$ attend only to the prefix before slot $j$ and to their own earlier
tokens; later tokens attend to the incumbent and to all candidates through gated attention
\begin{equation}
a_{tu}=\frac{G_{tu}\exp(s_{tu}-m_t)}{\sum_{v}G_{tv}\exp(s_{tv}-m_t)},\qquad G_{tu}=g_{\sigma(u)},
\label{eq:gate}
\end{equation}
where $\sigma(u)$ is the segment (incumbent block or candidate) of key $u$; because the gate
multiplies after the max-shift, $g=0$ removes a key exactly. Each slot carries a continuous offset $\omega_j$ that moves every later
token by $\Omega_t=\sum_j M_{tj}\omega_j$ positions, realized as an extra rotary rotation of
queries and keys (rotary scores depend only on position differences).

\begin{proposition}[Exact vertices]
\label{prop:vertices}
At gates $1$ on incumbents and $0$ on candidates, and $\omega=0$, every prompt and tail token has
its hidden states in $f_\theta(P(x)\oplus\text{tail})$. Choosing per slot one of
\{keep, delete, candidate $c$\}, setting the chosen candidate's gate to $1$, the replaced
incumbent's to $0$, and $\omega_j=|c|-|b_j|$ (or $-|b_j|$, or $|c|$ for an insertion), the
superposed computation equals $f_\theta$ on the correspondingly edited prompt, for any number of
simultaneous edits of any lengths.
\end{proposition}

The proof is by induction over layers (Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}); we verify it numerically in
fp32 for Llama, Gemma-2 (with logit soft-capping) and Qwen3 architectures, including combined
edits and tempered likelihoods (Appendix~\ref{app:tests}). The vertices of \greater{}'s one-hot
relaxation are real tokens; ours are real structured prompts of any length.

\paragraph{One pass for all candidates.} First-order gate derivatives saturate: the linear
extrapolation of an attention row overshoots the actual change by the factor $1+S_c/Z_0$, the
ratio of the candidate's attention mass to the existing mass, which is large for instruction
blocks (Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}). \emph{Renormalized patching} instead recomputes, from the
exact keys and values present in the superposed pass, each downstream attention output with slot
$j$ swapped to an alternative $a$ (query rotated by the length change) and linearizes only the
propagation through later layers,
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\Delta\ell}(a)=\sum_{\text{layers}}\sum_{t\text{ after slot }j}\big\langle\nabla_{o_t}\ell,\;o_t(a)-o_t(b_j)\big\rangle ,
\label{eq:patch}
\end{equation}
the AtP$^*$ principle \citep{kramar2024atp} applied to prompt edits. One forward and one backward
pass score every single-slot edit of a sequence; we use it to estimate the likelihood ratios of
\S\ref{sec:method} and report its fidelity against exact scoring.
