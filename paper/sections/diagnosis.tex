\section{Does the signal predict what structural edits do?}
\label{sec:diagnosis}

\paragraph{Design.} In each pool a model proposes $20$--$21$ label-free rewrites and insertions for
the slots of its incumbent prompt. Predictors, computed on $24$ training questions, are the
fixed-reasoning objective (exact and by one-pass patching) and, as reference, fresh greedy accuracy
on $8$ and on all $24$ questions; the target is the change in greedy accuracy on $200$ held-out
questions. Eight pools replicate on
Llama-3-8B and Qwen3-8B (three tasks, one non-initial prompt state) under a protocol fixed before
their data for the fork hypothesis H-fork, which failed (Appendix~\ref{app:prereg}); three test an
untouched task prospectively (TS3, three models). Pools are the units of inference (sign counts,
exact sign-flip tests, made primary after these data were collected); bootstrap intervals are
conditional on the pools. Best-3 regret---the held-out change of the three truly best edits minus
that of a predictor's top three---measures what a shortlist needs, against the exact expectation for
a random shortlist; it replaces the registered best-5, under which the conclusions are unchanged.

\paragraph{The fixed-reasoning objective ranks block edits in the wrong direction.}
Computed exactly, it correlates negatively with the held-out change in $8$ of $11$ pools (pooled
Spearman $-0.26$; sign-flip $p=0.017$; Table~\ref{tab:validity}); its one-pass estimate tracks the
exact values (mean Spearman $0.79$) and is negative in $9$ of $11$ ($-0.20$; $p=0.037$). Fresh
accuracy on the same questions is positive in $9$ ($8$ questions; $+0.28$, $p=0.010$) and $10$
($24$ questions; $+0.32$, $p=0.004$) of the $11$ pools. As a shortlist the patched objective is
worse than a random one in $8$ of $11$ pools (best-3 regret $0.091$ vs.\ $0.067$; $p=0.022$); the
exact one is not distinguishable from random ($0.075$; worse in $7$ of $11$, $p=0.14$), and on the
untouched task its correlation is weaker ($-0.13$, negative in two of three pools; patched $-0.20$,
negative in all three). The mean correlation is negative for every model and task, though its size
varies, and it is negative on the two pools with little sample truncation as well
(\S\ref{sec:limitations}). On wrongly
answered questions the negative correlation persists for Llama-3 but not for Qwen3
(Appendix~\ref{app:wrongrows}); two preregistered repairs failed (Appendix~\ref{app:prereg}).

\paragraph{Even a single-token edit changes most greedy reasoning.} We re-read the token-level control
(Llama-3 and Qwen3 on LD7 and TS7, $24$ \greater{}-style single-token substitutions per pool;
Appendix~\ref{app:results}) with an engine whose same-prompt re-reads are token-identical
(Appendix~\ref{app:reader}). On average, one substitution changes the greedy reasoning on $79$--$93\%$ of
$200$ held-out questions, first diverging after a median of $42.5$--$164$ tokens, and flips
correctness on $15$--$20\%$ (Table~\ref{tab:reroll}, Appendix~\ref{app:reroll}). Reproducible
reads are not batch-invariant
\citep{yuan2025nondeterminism,he2025nondeterminism}: the unchanged incumbent read in requests of
$16$ to $100$ questions changes the reasoning on $58$--$85\%$ of questions and flips correctness on
$10$--$16\%$. With these numerical re-rolls as the null (exploratory, specified after seeing the
descriptive numbers), no pool shows a significant difference in mean accuracy between edits and
re-rolls (exact permutation $p=0.07$--$0.88$; pooled $p=0.31$), although edits flip correctness on
more questions in three of four pools. Greedy accuracy on $200$ questions thus
has a numerical floor of $1$--$2$ points (sd), even with a deterministic engine; single-token
edits sit at it, where no predictor is distinguishable from zero (Table~\ref{tab:validity-token}),
while block edits move held-out accuracy by $2.1$--$10.9$ points on average.
