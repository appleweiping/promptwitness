\title{What Gradients over Reasoning Miss:\\ Prompt Edits Rewrite the Reasoning}

\author{Anonymous authors\\Paper under double-blind review}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
\greater{} lets a small language model optimize its own prompt with the gradient of the answer
loss over its own reasoning, generated once and held fixed. Does this signal predict what prompt
edits do? We make the objective exact for edits that rewrite, delete or insert whole instructions,
by superposing candidate blocks behind attention gates, and test it with three \mbox{8--9B} models
on eleven pools of their own block edits, collected under a protocol fixed before their data. It
ranks these edits in the \emph{wrong} direction (negative in $8$ of $11$ pools, pooled Spearman
$-0.26$), while fresh greedy accuracy on a few questions points the right way. An exact
decomposition explains why: edits act through the reasoning the model writes, whereas the objective
sees only the change of the answer read off a given reasoning, a term that on the models' own
samples averages at most $1.2$ accuracy points per edit against mean absolute held-out effects of
$2.1$--$10.9$. Reweighting the model's samples by exact likelihood ratios fails its preregistered
test, as a coverage argument predicts: edits move the reasoning distribution beyond what the
samples cover. Even a single-token substitution changes the greedy reasoning on $79$--$93\%$ of
questions (numerical re-rolls of the unchanged prompt: $58$--$85\%$). Edits must therefore be
judged on fresh reasoning, where verification decides: in exploratory analyses, accepting the best
candidate on $16$ or fewer generations lowers held-out accuracy, and a noise-calibrated acceptance
margin limits this harm. \todo{end-to-end: structural search and official \greater{}, gradient vs.\
random shortlist}.
\end{abstract}

\subsection*{AI use statement}
% Authors: verify and adapt this statement before submission.
Generative AI was used extensively in this work. An autonomous coding agent (Claude, Anthropic),
directed by the authors, proposed and implemented the method, wrote the experiment and analysis
code, ran the experiments and drafted the text. Adversarial reviews of the idea and of intermediate
results were done by a model of a different family (GPT, OpenAI) in an early phase and later by
fresh-context instances of the same model family that read the code and raw results directly.
Related work was checked against the cited sources, and every numerical result is produced by
scripts that read the recorded run outputs. The authors reviewed all AI-assisted work and take
responsibility for the final content, including text, claims and artifacts.

\subsection*{Reproducibility statement}
Code for the superposed gated computation, the one-pass estimator, the distributional scores, the
search loop, all baselines and all analyses is provided as supplementary material and will be
released. Exactness propositions are proved in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs} and verified numerically
(Appendix~\ref{app:tests}); data splits are deterministic functions of the public \greater{}
release, and the released records list the example identifiers of every split, including the
questions used to form the clean subset; the preregistered experiment plan with its dated
amendments and all run records are included.
